A three-note research sequence

Certified Coverage of Exponential Search Spaces

Jonathan R. Landers · September–October 2026

From a general coverage criterion, through a concrete stress test, to a probability question with certified bounds.

The question. A search over \(n\) binary choices has \(2^n\) candidate states. Can exact summaries or certified exclusions account for enough of them that the remaining search is small? Can we obtain those summaries cheaply enough to help?

The key formalism

In Note I, \(n\) is the number of binary choices and \(C(n)\) counts states handled by exact representation or certified exclusion. The residual \(r(n)\) must still be searched explicitly. Let \(T(n)\) be the modeled runtime; coverage overhead includes the work of discovering, representing, and certifying covered states:

\[ r(n)=2^n-C(n),\qquad T(n)=\text{coverage overhead}+r(n). \]

Within this model, polynomial overhead leads to polynomial runtime exactly when the residual is polynomially bounded. Covering a large fixed percentage of \(2^n\) states is not enough.

Note III changes the outcome being measured. Fix a list of weights \(w=(w_1,\ldots,w_n)\), and let \(A(w)\) be its set of attainable subset sums. Draw a target \(T\) from a specified distribution \(\mu\). Let \(L\) contain witnessed reachable targets and \(U\) every target not soundly excluded. For any target set \(B\), let \(\mu(B)\) mean the probability that \(T\in B\):

\[ q_\mu(w)=\Pr_{T\sim\mu}\!\bigl[T\in A(w)\bigr], \qquad \mu(L)\le q_\mu(w)\le\mu(U). \]

The bounds hold for a particular weight list; their width measures how much remains unresolved about the random-target question.

What the results say

Representation must earn its cost. The paired subset-sum solver was slower than meet-in-the-middle on all 18 constructed hard no-solution cases tested.

Prediction helps choose work. The classifier's held-out area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), a ranking measure, was 0.866, close to a simple density model's 0.864. Two of its 43 most confident NO proposals were actually YES, so exact elimination still needs a verifier.

Probability has a precise meaning. For a fixed target, subset sum remains YES or NO. A probability arises after specifying how a target is drawn. On 50 held-out 16-item lists, simple certified bounds contained the exact reachable-target fraction but left a substantial gap.
