About the data, labels and projection

What “easy” and “hard” mean

Easy inputs were completed exactly by the original solver within an 8L work budget. Hard inputs remained unresolved under that budget. This is a measured solver outcome, not a proof of intrinsic mathematical hardness.

Nine unscaled progression-class cases have a known easy structure but were not completed by that solver. Their color and cross preserve both facts. “Other completed” inputs do not establish a new polynomial-time class.

Five structures, overlapping memberships

Colors identify certified full-input structure: superincreasing weights, complete coverage, at most two distinct values, small reachable support, or a compact interval / arithmetic-progression representation.

Types can overlap. Click a point for every exact membership. Color prefers the matching intended generator type, then coordinate order. A generating-family name alone is never treated as a certificate, and empty reduced kernels do not create spurious memberships.

A view of the inputs, not a new model

The default is 576 targets from 144 weight lists. Targets sharing a list are dependent. Optional scaled encodings preserve mathematical answers while changing solver budgets and representation.

PCA uses the saved features only, standardized and fitted on unscaled inputs. The first three components retain the reported share of variance. Projection can overlap different inputs; it does not prove separation. The original-axes view offers directly readable properties. No completion predictor has been refitted.
